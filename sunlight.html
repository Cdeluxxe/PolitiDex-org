Sunlight Governance

Sunlight Governance tracks how regulated monopolies — such as electric, gas and water utilities — are overseen, by putting rate cases, regulator decisions and related lobbying records into one public, sourced place. The working proof-of-concept is the Philippines Digital Bayanihan Chain, which shows how public records of this kind can be published openly so anyone can follow them.

What can carry this tag

  • Issues about how a regulated utility is overseen
  • Bills that change utility regulation or a commission's process
  • Rate cases before a state public service or utilities commission
  • Lobbying records tied to a regulated utility

What is confidential or redacted

The public order does not state what share of the filing is confidential. It does name specific parts of the record that are designated confidential or redacted, listed below. Testimony in the docket is posted in redacted public versions.

  • Wind contract pricing.
    “Because the pricing of the PPAs and the costs associated with the other Wind Projects are designated confidential, the PSC discusses these issues in general terms.” p. 103
  • Generation overhaul adjustments.
    “They argue that RMP should remove the converted units from its four-year calculation, each recommending a confidential adjustment to correct the issue.” p. 89
  • Injuries and damages claims data.
    “DPU acknowledges RMP provided data on the origin of claims but notes the information is heavily redacted, precluding reasonable evaluation.” p. 88
  • Load forecast model variables.
    “much information is designated confidential in GRCs and RMP may avail itself of existing processes to protect any confidential information.” p. 59
  • Insurer underwriting exhibit.
    “RMP also provided a confidential exhibit it requested one of its insurers to prepare, which discusses the insurer’s underwriting process” p. 147

What the Office of Consumer Services argued

The Office of Consumer Services (OCS), which represents residential and small business customers before the PSC, was a party to the case.

  • Recommended a 9.375% return on equity, below the 9.65% Rocky Mountain Power requested. The PSC adopted 9.375%, finding the OCS recommendation reasonable under the principle of gradualism. p. 21
  • Asked the PSC to disallow at least $15,289,781 of claimed wildfire-related deferred costs: $5,681,724 in labor costs OCS argued were already recovered through base rates, and $9,608,057 spent under a 2023 wildfire plan the PSC had not approved. p. 131
  • Joined other parties in opposing full recovery of projected excess liability insurance premiums, arguing the utility had not provided substantial evidence that the full amount was prudent. p. 148
  • Argued the allocation factor used to spread those insurance premiums across states “does not assign the costs to the jurisdictions in proportion to the amount each jurisdiction contributes to the increase,” violating cost-causation principles. p. 158
  • Recommended removing two Jim Bridger units converted to natural gas, and Craig Unit 2, from the generation overhaul expense average; the amount of its adjustment is confidential. p. 89

The ask: Publish one confidential contract.

Ask Rocky Mountain Power to file a public version of one confidential contract in this docket: the pricing terms of the Cedar Springs IV wind power purchase agreement. The utility holds the contract, so the burden of showing why any term must stay confidential rests with the utility. This is a request about one existing document. It does not ask for any new law.
