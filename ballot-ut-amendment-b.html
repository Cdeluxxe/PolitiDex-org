Utah · Statewide ballot question · November 3, 2026

Constitutional Amendment B

A 60% vote for citizen initiatives that raise taxes · S.J.R. 2, 2025 General Session · amends Utah Constitution, Article VI, Section 1

Official ballot language

“Shall the Utah Constitution be amended to require that citizen-proposed laws that increase taxes compared to existing law be approved by at least 60% of voters, instead of a simple majority?” Constitutional Amendment B, as it appears on the 2026 Utah general-election ballot.

What your vote does

A Yes vote means a statewide citizen initiative voted on after November 1, 2026 needs at least 60% of the votes cast on it, instead of a simple majority, if it imposes a new tax, expands an existing tax to more items or transactions, increases an existing tax rate, or changes a property tax rate so that it decreases less than it would under current law. It does not apply to initiatives that only spend money, or to tax changes passed by the Legislature. It takes effect November 23, 2026.

A No vote leaves the simple-majority rule in place for those initiatives: a statewide citizen initiative that raises a tax continues to pass with a majority of those voting on it.

The text change

S.J.R. 2 adds one new subsection to Utah Constitution, Article VI, Section 1, directly after the existing two-thirds rule for wildlife initiatives. Nothing is removed.

(iii) Notwithstanding Subsection (2)(a)(i)(A), initiated legislation that is subject to a vote that occurs after November 1, 2026, shall be adopted upon approval of at least 60% of those voting on the legislation if the initiated legislation includes:

(A) the imposition of a new tax;

(B) an expansion of an existing tax to include additional items or transactions subject to the tax;

(C) an increase in an existing tax rate; or

(D) for a property tax, a change to the tax rate that causes the tax rate to decrease less than it would under current law.

Underlined text is added. Subsection (2)(a)(i)(A), the provision it overrides for these initiatives, reads that voters may “initiate any desired legislation and cause it to be submitted to the people for adoption upon a majority vote of those voting on the legislation.” Taken from the enrolled copy of S.J.R. 2.

Background

How initiatives pass today

Article VI, Section 1 gives Utah voters the power to propose laws by initiative and adopt them by a majority of those voting. There is one existing exception: since a 1998 amendment, an initiative to allow, limit or prohibit the taking of wildlife needs two-thirds of the vote. Amendment B would add a second exception, for initiatives that raise taxes.

An example from the record

Proposition 3 in 2018, the citizen initiative that expanded Medicaid, paid for itself with a statewide sales-tax increase. It passed with 53.3% of the vote. A measure built the same way would need 60% under Amendment B.

What it does not cover

  • Tax changes passed by the Legislature. Those still pass with a majority of each house.
  • Initiatives that spend money without imposing, expanding or raising a tax.
  • County, city and town initiatives. Subsection (2)(b), which governs them, is unchanged.
  • Referendums on laws the Legislature has passed.
  • Statewide initiatives voted on or before November 1, 2026.
