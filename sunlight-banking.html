Sunlight Banking

Credit is still created, but the spread and outcomes are visible and biased toward local use instead of distant shareholders.

What the public record already says

  • The FDIC insures deposits up to $250,000 per depositor, per insured bank, for each account ownership category — a public guarantee behind every insured bank.
  • The Utah Department of Financial Institutions charters and supervises Utah state-chartered banks, credit unions and industrial banks.
  • Utah charters more industrial banks (industrial loan companies) than any other state.
  • Public money held by Utah’s state and local treasurers is governed by the State Money Management Act and overseen by the Money Management Council.

Minimum viable ledger

The smallest set of lines that makes this ledger useful. Each line is filled from a public source below.

  • Public deposits (city, county, state): which banks and credit unions hold them.
  • Loans originated or participated in by any public or public-partner bank.
  • Borrower type (contractor, small business, housing, farm and so on), amount, rate and term.
  • Real outcomes: jobs created, units built, projects completed, repayments and defaults.
  • Ownership of the lender: shareholder-owned bank, credit union or public bank.
  • PAC donations, and how legislators voted on banking bills.

Market-Power Test: Banking

Three questions to ask of any company or sector. Each one is answered from public records, not opinion.

  1. Choice. Can a customer or taxpayer walk away and use a real alternative?
  2. Concentration. Do a few firms hold most of the market in this place?
  3. Public backing. Does public money, a public charter or a public guarantee stand behind it?

The more of these the record answers “no choice”, “a few firms” or “yes, public backing”, the more a full public ledger helps people see the whole picture.

Utah starting moves

  1. Redirect even one Utah city’s or county’s deposits from the big four banks into credit unions or a future public option.
  2. Model a Utah public bank after North Dakota’s: it holds public deposits, partners with community banks and credit unions on loans (it does not compete for retail customers), and returns its surplus to the state and local level.
  3. Track every loan on the ledger so the next county can copy what works.
