Sunlight Concrete

Sunlight Concrete puts the public record of concrete, cement and gravel supply for public works in one open place, so anyone can see who bids, who wins and who owns the pits and plants nearby.

What the public record already says

  • Ready-mixed concrete is generally placed within about 90 minutes of mixing under the common ASTM C94 specification, so it is sold close to where it is made.
  • Sand, gravel and crushed stone are heavy and costly to haul, so each area’s supply comes mostly from nearby pits and quarries.
  • Public agencies, including the Utah Department of Transportation and cities and counties, are major buyers of concrete and aggregate for roads, bridges and buildings.
  • Public bids, contracts and payments are public records in Utah under the Government Records Access and Management Act (GRAMA).

Minimum viable ledger

The smallest set of lines that makes this ledger useful. Each line is filled from a public source below.

  • Bids and awards: who bid and who won on public concrete and paving work.
  • Public payments: what state agencies paid each supplier and contractor.
  • Supply sites: the permitted pits, quarries, cement and ready-mix plants in each area, and their owners.
  • Concentration: how many independent suppliers serve each area.
  • Unit prices: public bid prices per unit of concrete and aggregate over time.

Market-Power Test: Concrete

Three questions to ask of any company or sector. Each one is answered from public records, not opinion.

  1. Choice. Can a customer or taxpayer walk away and use a real alternative?
  2. Concentration. Do a few firms hold most of the market in this place?
  3. Public backing. Does public money, a public charter or a public guarantee stand behind it?

The more of these the record answers “no choice”, “a few firms” or “yes, public backing”, the more a full public ledger helps people see the whole picture.

Utah starting moves

  1. Pull a recent year of UDOT bid results and list who bid and who won on concrete paving and structures.
  2. Map the permitted gravel pits, quarries and concrete plants from Utah’s public air-quality approval orders and mine permits, by county.
  3. For one county, file one ledger page: the supply sites and their owners, the public bids and awards, and the state payments, each linked to its source.

Public data sources

Public records and public datasets only. Nothing here asks for or holds private account data.

  • Utah Department of Transportation · construction bid results and awarded contracts
  • Transparent Utah · state and local government payments to vendors
  • Utah Division of Purchasing · state contracts and solicitations
  • Utah Division of Air Quality · approval orders for pits, quarries and plants
  • Utah Division of Oil, Gas and Mining · mine permits
  • USGS Cement statistics · national and state cement figures
  • USGS Construction sand and gravel statistics · national and state aggregate figures
